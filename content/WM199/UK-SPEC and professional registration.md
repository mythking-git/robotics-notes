## The idea
In the UK, "engineer" isn't a protected title, but **Chartered Engineer** is. The **Engineering Council** sets the standard for professional registration in a document called **UK-SPEC**, and licenses professional institutions (like the **IET** and **IMechE**) to assess people against it. Registration is independent proof that you're competent, which matters most where the public's safety depends on your work, as it does with robots.

## The three titles
| Title | Typical route | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **EngTech** (Engineering Technician) | Apprenticeship or work experience | Applying proven techniques |
| **IEng** (Incorporated Engineer) | BEng plus experience | Managing and applying existing technology |
| **CEng** (Chartered Engineer) | BEng plus further learning (or an MEng), plus experience | Developing new technology, leading, innovating |

All three are assessed against the **same five areas**, at different depths.

## The five competence areas
| Area | In short |
|---|---|
| **A · Knowledge and understanding** | The science, maths and engineering behind your field, applied |
| **B · Design, development and problem solving** | Turning knowledge into designs that solve real problems |
| **C · Responsibility, management and leadership** | Planning, managing resources, leading or contributing in a team |
| **D · Communication and interpersonal skills** | Explaining technical ideas to technical and non-technical people |
| **E · Professional commitment** | A code of conduct, safety, sustainability and lifelong learning |

## Why it matters in robotics
- **Safety-critical systems:** robots share spaces with people.
- **Accountability:** who's responsible when an AI system makes a decision? Bias, transparency and duty of care all apply.
- **Public trust:** registration shows independently checked competence.

## Degrees: AHEP
UK-SPEC covers people. Its partner standard, **AHEP**, covers degrees: the 18 learning outcomes an accredited degree has to teach. Every module on this course is tagged against it. See [[Accreditation|accreditation, AHEP and the IET]].

## Engineering identity
How you see yourself as an engineer, shaped by your education, your experience and your values. It isn't fixed: a degree is where you start building it. A useful exercise is rating yourself 1–5 against each area **with evidence** for each score, then picking the gaps to work on (see [[CPD and reflective practice]]).
