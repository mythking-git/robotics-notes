## Overview
**Robotics Engineering with Artificial Intelligence (BEng)** at **WMG, University of Warwick** is a three-year degree that combines mechanical, electrical and software engineering with AI, aimed at designing and building robotic systems. 2026–27 is the first year it has run. WMG plans to seek **IET accreditation** for it (see [[Accreditation|accreditation and AHEP]]), and there's an optional year in industry or research.

## How Year 1 is structured
Year 1 is **120 credits**, all core. You have to pass every module (pass mark 40%), but Year 1 marks don't count towards your final degree classification. Years 2 and 3 count 30% and 70%.

Instead of running every module all year, the course is taught in **blocks**: three modules at a time, each with its own lectures, seminars and labs.

| Block | When | Modules | Credits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Oct – early Dec | [[WM1AD/index\|WM1AD]] Electrical Power and Motors · [[WM1AC/index\|WM1AC]] Mechanisms and Machines · [[WM199/index\|WM199]] Developing an Engineering Mindset | 15 each |
| **2** | Dec – early Mar | [[WM1AA/index\|WM1AA]] Rapid Prototyping · [[WM1AE/index\|WM1AE]] Programmable Electronics · [[WM1AF/index\|WM1AF]] Engineering Systems Design | 15 each |
| **3** | Mar – May | [[WM1AB/index\|WM1AB]] Mechatronics Sprint Project | 30 |

The first two blocks build the toolkit: circuits and motors, mechanics, professional skills, prototyping, microcontrollers and systems design. Block 3 puts it all together in a **team project**: design, build and test a robot for the end-of-year competition.

### A typical block
- **Weeks 1–4:** teaching. Each module usually has two lectures and a seminar a week, plus labs every other week.
- **Week 5:** first assessment week, no teaching.
- **Weeks 6–7:** more teaching and the last labs.
- **Week 9:** second assessment week, for the reports.

## How assessment works
Most modules have two assessments:

| | Weight | Usually | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A1** | 30% | A timed computer-based test on campus (calculations plus short written answers), or a short individual portfolio or report | Week 5 of the block |
| **A2** | 70% | A longer individual report, often built on the lab work | Week 9 of the block |

- **You must pass every component**, not just the module overall.
- In the tests, a formula sheet is usually provided and only basic scientific calculators are allowed, so practise with the calculator you'll use. Written working is often marked.
- Group work in Blocks 2 and 3 is **peer-assessed**.
- Each assessment brief says whether AI tools are allowed.

## Study tips that have worked for me
- Read the lab section of the brief **before** each lab, and photograph every reading. Your report depends on the data you take that day.
- Finish every circuit calculation with a **power balance** and every kinematics answer with a **units check**.
- Do the problem sets the week they're set. The tests in week 5 cover everything from weeks 1–4.
