## The usual stages
1. **Online application:** CV, sometimes a cover letter or short questions ("Why this company?")
2. **Online tests:** see below
3. **Video interview:** recorded answers to on-screen questions, usually a minute or two each
4. **Assessment centre or final interview:** group exercise, technical interview, presentation, competency interview

Not every company uses every stage, especially smaller ones.

## Online tests
| Test | What it checks | How to prepare |
|---|---|---|
| **Numerical reasoning** | Reading tables and charts, percentages, ratios | Practise timed; most tests allow a calculator |
| **Verbal reasoning** | True / false / cannot say from a passage | Answer only from the text, not what you know |
| **Logical / diagrammatic** | Patterns in shapes | Practice sets; they get faster with familiarity |
| **Situational judgement** | Choosing the best action in workplace scenarios | Read the company's values first |
| **Game-based** | Attention, risk-taking, memory | Can't really be revised; just be rested |

Free practice tests are available from many test providers and careers services.

## Answering competency questions: STAR
"Tell me about a time when you…" questions want a real story:
| | |
|---|---|
| **S**ituation | Brief context: where, when |
| **T**ask | What you had to do |
| **A**ction | What **you** did (say "I", not "we"), and why |
| **R**esult | What happened, with numbers if possible, and what you learned |

Prepare 6–8 stories that you can bend to fit common themes: teamwork, leadership, a problem you solved, a mistake, a deadline, persuading someone, something technical you're proud of. University labs, group projects, part-time jobs, societies and personal builds all count.

## Technical interviews
For engineering roles, expect to be asked to explain something from your course or your own projects. Be ready to talk through **one project in depth**: what it does, why you designed it that way, what went wrong, and what you'd change. Diagrams on a whiteboard or paper help.

## Assessment centres
- **Group exercise:** they're watching how you work with others, not just whether you "win". Bring quieter people in, keep time, summarise.
- **Presentation:** short and structured. Answer the question asked.
- **Questions for them:** have two genuine ones ready.

## Check yourself
> [!question]- 1. Turn "we built a robot for a module" into a STAR answer outline.
> S: first-year group project to build a line-following robot · T: I was responsible for the motor control · A: I chose the motor driver, wrote the PWM code and tested it against the speed requirement, and changed the gearing when it was too slow · R: it hit the target speed and we came third out of twelve; I learned to test early.
