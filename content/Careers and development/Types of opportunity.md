## The idea
Most engineering employers recruit students through a few standard schemes. Knowing which ones are open to you **this year** matters, because the shorter ones are aimed at first-years, and many close months before they start.

| Type | Length | Usually for | When you apply |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Insight day / open day** | 1 day | Any year | Through the year, often autumn and spring |
| **Spring week** | 1–5 days, around Easter | **First-years** (some second-years) | Autumn to early winter of first year |
| **Summer internship** | 8–12 weeks, paid | Mostly penultimate-year students, some earlier | Autumn to winter before the summer |
| **Placement year (year in industry)** | 9–15 months, paid | Second-years, taken between years 2 and 3 | Autumn to winter of second year |
| **Graduate scheme** | 1–3 years | Final-years and graduates | Autumn of final year |
| **Degree apprenticeship** | 3–5 years, paid, degree included | Usually school leavers; some graduate-level schemes | Varies; many in autumn and spring |

Dates differ by company, and **many applications are rolling**: they close early once enough people have applied. Apply as soon as something opens.

## Spring weeks and insight days
Short programmes to show you what a company does. They're designed for people with **no experience**, so first year is exactly the right time. Some lead to a fast-track or guaranteed interview for an internship or placement the next year.

## Summer internships
Real project work over the summer. Engineering firms mostly target students a year or two from graduating, but smaller companies, start-ups and research labs often take first-years. University research internships (working with a lecturer over the summer) are another route.

## Placement year
This degree offers an optional year in industry. You'd usually apply in the autumn of Year 2 and go between Years 2 and 3. A placement is often the strongest thing on a graduate CV, and many lead to a graduate job offer.

## Degree apprenticeships
You work for an employer and study for a degree part-time, with fees paid by the employer and a salary. They're mostly entered straight from school, so for current undergraduates they're more relevant as:
- something to know about if you're thinking of changing route (talk to the university first: you'd usually start again from Year 1)
- **graduate-level and master's apprenticeships** that some employers offer after a degree.

Search the government's **Find an apprenticeship** service and company careers pages.
