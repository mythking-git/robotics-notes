## The short version
- **AHEP** is the Engineering Council's standard for what an engineering degree has to teach. It's a list of **18 learning outcomes**.
- The **IET** (Institution of Engineering and Technology) is licensed by the Engineering Council to check degrees against AHEP and **accredit** them.
- An accredited BEng (Hons) **partly meets** the education requirement for **Chartered Engineer (CEng)**. You'd still need further learning and work experience later.
- Every module on this course is written against AHEP. Each module learning outcome is tagged with the AHEP outcome it covers, e.g. *[AHEP:4-C9]*. Year 1 already touches 17 of the 18.
- The course is new, so **it isn't accredited yet**. WMG plans to seek accreditation, but it can't be guaranteed until it's granted. Our cohort's work is part of the evidence.

> [!warning] Not official
> This is my own summary from the module documents, the Warwick course page and the IET and Engineering Council guidance (October 2026). Check the course handbook or ask WMG for the official position.

## How the pieces fit together
```
Engineering Council
 ├── UK-SPEC ── what a registered ENGINEER must show  (competence areas A–E)
 └── AHEP ───── what an accredited DEGREE must teach   (learning outcomes 1–18)
        │
        ▼
 IET (licensed institution)
 ├── accredits degree programmes against AHEP
 └── registers people against UK-SPEC (EngTech · IEng · CEng)
```

AHEP and UK-SPEC are two halves of the same system. AHEP covers the **education** part of becoming registered. UK-SPEC covers the **competence** you show afterwards at work. See [[UK-SPEC and professional registration]] for the five competence areas.

## The AHEP learning outcomes
AHEP 4th edition (2020) has 18 outcomes, in five groups. Each has three versions:
- **C1–C18:** Bachelors (Hons), partly meeting CEng. **This is the set our course uses.**
- **M1–M18:** integrated MEng, fully meeting CEng.
- **B1–B18:** Bachelors, fully meeting IEng.

The C and M versions talk about **complex** problems, and the B versions about **broadly-defined** ones.

## How accreditation will happen for this course
- **Status:** Warwick says WMG *aims to seek* accreditation that partly meets the CEng education requirement, and that it can't be guaranteed at this stage. WMG's established degrees are already accredited.
- **When:** the IET won't accredit a new BEng until its final year is running, and normally looks at **graduate outputs**, mainly final-year project reports. For us, that means around 2028–29.
- **Backdating:** when a programme is accredited for the first time after producing graduates, the IET can **backdate** accreditation to cover earlier intakes, as long as there's evidence of those cohorts' outputs. That's how first cohorts are normally covered.
- **Since 2022–23,** accreditation for IEng as well as partial CEng isn't automatic. The IET has to see both sets of outcomes mapped separately.
- **Keep an eye on:** the handbook rules on progression. Some modules carry conditions on staying on the accredited version of the course.

## What this means for you
- **Keep your work.** Save every submitted assessment, its feedback, and your lab data. As the first cohort, our work is the evidence for accreditation, and it's your own record too.
- **Read the AHEP tags on each brief.** They tell you what the assessment is really testing. A brief tagged *C9* wants a proper risk process, not just a list of hazards.
- **Start a CPD log now** ([[CPD log template]]). It covers C18 and is exactly what the IET asks for when you apply for registration later.
- **Consider IET student membership.** It's cheap for the whole course, and it gives you events, a mentor network and an earlier start on the route to registration.

## The route to Chartered Engineer
```
Accredited BEng (Hons)  ──►  Further learning  ──►  Experience at work  ──►  Professional review  ──►  CEng
  (partial CEng, AHEP C)      (accredited MSc, or      (show UK-SPEC A–E,      (application + interview
                               learning at work)         usually 4–7 years)      with two CEngs)
```
An accredited BEng on its own leads more directly to **IEng**, if the degree is accredited for it. CEng needs Master's-level learning on top: an accredited MSc, an MEng if WMG adds one, or further learning assessed at work.
